This lesson is for arts and humanities researchers who want to use AI with books, images, archives and other material protected by copyright. It is also for the librarians, data service staff and research support teams who help them. No technical background is needed.

The lesson is an output of the [Connecting Arts and Humanities Infrastructures for AI Capability](https://artsandhumanities.ai) project funded by UKRI under IS8.

By the end of the lesson learners will be able to think through copyright when using AI in their research, including:
- what does copyright mean when I use AI?
- can I put this material through AI?
- what happens when I upload material to an AI tool?
- who owns what the AI produces?
- how do I share my data and results?

Hands-on exercises help them make informed, responsible choices about using AI with material protected by copyright, licences and terms of use.

The lesson gives general information, not legal advice. Learners are pointed to their institution's copyright and research support teams for advice on their own projects.

The lesson has five short episodes of about 25 minutes each (10 minutes teaching and 15 minutes exercises). Each episode stands alone, so they can be taught separately or in any order.
